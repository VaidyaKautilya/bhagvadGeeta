# Bhagavad Gita

## Index and Beginning of the Book

This book is a complete reading of the **Bhagavad Gita** in **18 chapters** and **701 shlokas**.

It is written for a person who is starting. You do not need Sanskrit, and you do not need to know the Mahabharata first. Read this index once. Then open Chapter 1.

The Gita is a conversation on the battlefield of Kurukshetra. The warrior **Arjuna** will not fight. His guide **Krishna** teaches him. **Sanjaya** tells the blind king **Dhritarashtra** what is said. The war is the setting. The teaching is about how to live, act, love, know, and take refuge.

## How to Read Each Shloka

Every verse in this book is written in the **same five parts**. Learn this page, and every chapter will be clear.

| Order | Heading in the file | What it is | How to use it |
| :---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Sanskrit** | The original shloka | See the verse as it stands. You may read it aloud or skip the letters at first. |
| 2 | **Simple Meaning** | Easy English | Read this first. It is the plain sense of the verse. |
| 3 | **Gujarati** | Simple Gujarati translation | The same sense in Gujarati, close to the words. |
| 4 | **ગુજરાતી ગહન અર્થ** | Deeper meaning | Unpacks key Sanskrit words and joins this verse to the chapter. |
| 5 | **આ શ્લોક શું શીખવે છે** | What it teaches | The lesson to take into life. One clear teaching per verse. |

**A simple way to start:**

1. Read **Simple Meaning**.
2. Read **What it teaches**.
3. If you want more, read the Gujarati and the deeper meaning.
4. Look at the Sanskrit when you are ready.

Each chapter also has, before the verses:

- **Chapter Overview** — name, shloka count, speakers, setting
- **What This Chapter Is About** — the teaching in short
- **Simple Story** — a picture for a beginner
- **The Background Story** — how this chapter follows the last one

And at the end:

- **Simple Summary** — what happened, why it matters, the big question

Do not try to finish the whole book in one sitting. One chapter, or even a few shlokas, is enough.

## Before the Teaching: The War

You do not need the whole Mahabharata. You need this much:

Two cousin-families claim one throne. The **Pandavas** (five brothers: Yudhishthira, Bhima, Arjuna, Nakula, Sahadeva) have been wronged. The **Kauravas**, led by **Duryodhana**, son of the blind king Dhritarashtra, will not give the kingdom back. The armies meet at **Kurukshetra**, called both a field of war and a field of dharma.

**Krishna** drives Arjuna's chariot. He has promised not to lift a weapon. He will only guide. When the conches sound, Arjuna asks Krishna to place the chariot between the two armies. He sees grandfathers, teachers, cousins, and sons. His bow **Gandiva** slips. Chapter 1 is that falling. Chapter 2 is Krishna beginning to speak. Chapter 18 is Arjuna standing again, with the same bow, without the old fog.

## Words You Will Meet

These words return in almost every chapter. Learn them once here.

| Word | Simple sense in this book |
| --- | --- |
| **Shloka** | One verse. Numbered inside its chapter (for example 2.47 means Chapter 2, verse 47). |
| **Yoga** | A joining: a path that unites the person with the Highest. Each chapter is named a yoga. |
| **Dharma** | What is right to do; one's own duty. Not only "religion." |
| **Karma** | Action, and the binding that follows action. **Karma Yoga** is work without clinging to the fruit. |
| **Jnana** | Knowledge that sees. **Vijnana** is that knowledge lived. |
| **Bhakti** | Devotion; love and worship of God. |
| **Atman / Self** | The one who knows, inside. Not the body. |
| **Prakriti** | Nature; the field that acts. The three **gunas** are her strands. |
| **Gunas** | **Sattva** (clarity), **rajas** (restless passion), **tamas** (fog, sloth). They color faith, food, work, and joy. |
| **Tyaga** | Giving up the *fruit* of work, not the work itself. |
| **Sannyasa** | Laying down desire-born work; inward renunciation. |
| **Moksha** | Liberation; freedom from the round of binding. |
| **Svadharma** | One's own duty, born of one's nature. Better imperfect own-work than well-done borrowed work. |
| **Prasada** | Grace. The last chapters say the Highest is reached by grace as well as by effort. |

## The Three Parts of the Book

The 18 chapters fall into **three groups of six**. This is the map of the whole Gita.

| Part | Chapters | Path (category) | Shlokas | What this part is about |
| :---: | :---: | --- | ---: | --- |
| **I** | 1 to 6 | **Karma Yoga** — Action | 280 | Arjuna's grief. The soul. Duty without clinging to fruit. Work, renunciation, and meditation. |
| **II** | 7 to 12 | **Bhakti Yoga** — Devotion | 209 | Who God is. How to remember Him. His glories. The universal form. The devotee who is dear. |
| **III** | 13 to 18 | **Jnana Yoga** — Knowledge | 212 | Field and knower. The three gunas. The Supreme Person. Divine and demonic. Faith. Liberation and refuge. |

**Total: 18 chapters · 701 shlokas**

This count follows the edition used in these files. Chapter 13 includes Arjuna's opening question as a separate shloka (35 verses). Many printed Gitas count 700 by joining that question with the next verse.

## Chapter Guide for a Beginner

Read the chapters in order. Each one answers the last.

### Part I — Karma Yoga (Action) · Chapters 1–6 · 280 shlokas

**Chapter 1 — Arjuna Vishada Yoga · 47 shlokas**  
The armies stand. Arjuna sees his own people and puts down the bow. Grief, pity, and confusion begin the Gita. Without this sorrow, the teaching has no door.

**Chapter 2 — Sankhya Yoga · 72 shlokas**  
The longest teaching chapter after 18, and the seed of the whole book. The soul does not die. The body is worn like cloth. Do your duty. Do not cling to fruit. This is the standing of a steady mind.

**Chapter 3 — Karma Yoga · 43 shlokas**  
Arjuna asks: if knowledge is better, why must I act? Krishna says: you must act. Work as worship. Desire and anger are the enemies. Nature will make you act; act without the claim "I am the only doer."

**Chapter 4 — Jnana Karma Sanyasa Yoga · 42 shlokas**  
This yoga is ancient. Krishna takes birth to protect dharma. Many kinds of sacrifice. The fire of knowledge burns the binding of work. The knower of sacrifice is free.

**Chapter 5 — Karma Sanyasa Yoga · 29 shlokas**  
Is it better to leave work, or to work without clinging? Both can reach the same. Work with inner renunciation is kinder for most. The Self does not act. The sage sees one Lord in all.

**Chapter 6 — Dhyana Yoga · 47 shlokas**  
The yogi is the one who does the work that is his, without leaning on fruit. How to sit. How the mind is trained. What happens if a yogi falls. Krishna is the friend of all beings.

### Part II — Bhakti Yoga (Devotion) · Chapters 7–12 · 209 shlokas

**Chapter 7 — Jnana Vijnana Yoga · 30 shlokas**  
Krishna as the source of the world. Lower and higher nature. Four kinds of devotee. The wise one is dear. Few know Him as He is.

**Chapter 8 — Akshara Brahma Yoga · 28 shlokas**  
Arjuna asks about Brahman, the Self, karma, and how to go at death. What the mind holds at the last moment, that it becomes. Remember Him and go to the Imperishable.

**Chapter 9 — Raja Vidya Raja Guhya Yoga · 34 shlokas**  
The royal secret: the whole world is strung on Krishna like pearls on a thread. A leaf, a flower, a fruit, water, offered with love, He takes. Even the fallen who turn to Him are not lost.

**Chapter 10 — Vibhuti Yoga · 42 shlokas**  
Arjuna asks to hear the glories. Among lights, the sun; among warriors, Rama; among rivers, the Ganga. Whatever is excellent is a spark of His splendor.

**Chapter 11 — Vishvarupa Darshana Yoga · 55 shlokas**  
Arjuna is given the divine eye. He sees the universal form: many mouths, the worlds entering, time as the eater. He is afraid, then asks to see the gentle friend again. Undivided devotion is the way.

**Chapter 12 — Bhakti Yoga · 20 shlokas**  
The shortest teaching chapter. Form or the formless — which is better? Both reach Him; the path of love is kinder for a human body. A ladder of practice, then the portrait of the one who is dear: no hatred, a friend to all.

### Part III — Jnana Yoga (Knowledge) · Chapters 13–18 · 212 shlokas

**Chapter 13 — Kshetra Kshetrajna Vibhaga Yoga · 35 shlokas**  
This body is the field. The one who knows it is the knower. Krishna is the knower in every field. Humility and the list of real knowledge. Prakriti acts; the Self does not.

**Chapter 14 — Gunatraya Vibhaga Yoga · 27 shlokas**  
Sattva, rajas, tamas bind the embodied one. How they bind, how they show, where death in each leads. The one beyond the gunas. Unwavering devotion crosses them. Krishna is the ground of Brahman.

**Chapter 15 — Purushottama Yoga · 20 shlokas**  
The world as an inverted tree. Cut it with non-attachment. The jiva is an eternal fragment of Krishna. Two persons — perishable and imperishable — and the Supreme Person beyond both.

**Chapter 16 — Daivasura Sampad Vibhaga Yoga · 24 shlokas**  
Two endowments: divine and demonic. Fearlessness and truth on one side; hypocrisy, pride, and anger on the other. Three gates of hell: desire, anger, greed. Let scripture measure what to do.

**Chapter 17 — Shraddhatraya Vibhaga Yoga · 28 shlokas**  
Faith is not one color. It is sattvic, rajasic, or tamasic. So are food, sacrifice, austerity, and giving. Om Tat Sat. What is done without faith is asat — nothing here or after death.

**Chapter 18 — Moksha Sannyasa Yoga · 78 shlokas**  
The last and longest chapter. Sannyasa and tyaga. Keep the work; drop the fruit. Knowledge, action, doer, buddhi, joy — all sorted by the gunas. One's own dharma. The last word: take refuge in Me alone; I will free you from all sins; do not grieve. Arjuna's delusion is gone. He will do Krishna's word.

| Name | Role in this book |

## A Steady Pace

You do not have to read a whole chapter in one day.

| Pace | About how long for 701 shlokas |
| --- | --- |
| A few shlokas a day (5–10) | Two to four months |
| One chapter a day | A little over two weeks (Chapter 18 may take two days) |
| One part a week (six chapters) | Three weeks |

A good daily habit: read the **Simple Meaning** and **What it teaches** for as many verses as you can hold. Return the next day and read the Gujarati and the deeper meaning of the same verses. The Gita is a conversation. It rewards coming back.

If you stop, start again at the beginning of the chapter you left, not at verse 1 of the whole book, unless you want the grief of Chapter 1 to remind you why the teaching began.

## Begin Here

Open **[Chapter 1](Chapter%201.md)**.

The first verse is a blind king asking what his people and the Pandavas are doing on the field of dharma. That question is also yours: what is happening in my life, and what should I do?

Read the Simple Meaning. Read what the shloka teaches. Walk on. Krishna's answer begins in Chapter 2, and it does not end until Arjuna says: *I will do Your word.*
